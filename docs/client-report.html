What this costs in enquiries

Published industry research puts numbers against each of those gaps:

  • Visitors form a judgement of a company in 50 milliseconds, before reading a word, and 75% admit judging credibility by website design.1,2
  • 53% of mobile visits are abandoned if a page takes more than three seconds; bounce rates rise 32% between one and three seconds.3,4
  • Page speed is part of Google’s ranking system, and moving up one position lifts click-through by about 31%.5,6
  • B2B buyers spend only 17% of their evaluation time meeting suppliers — the rest is independent research, mostly online.7

For most EPC prospects, your website is the first meeting. An engineer shortlisting steel detailers at 11pm is comparing you against competitors on a screen, not in a room.

Two things that actively work against you today

Security. Slider Revolution is historically among the most exploited WordPress plugins — one 2014–15 vulnerability compromised over 100,000 sites. Outdated plugins remain the leading cause of WordPress compromise.8 A hacked site gets flagged by Google in search results, destroying exactly the trust this report is about.

Credibility details. The current homepage displays animated counters that read “0” next to Hours of Work, Clients and Staff, and the footer still says “Copyright 2023”. To a procurement auditor these read as an unmaintained business.

A note on typography

The current site uses Montserrat and Open Sans — the default pairing of a purchased template, loaded in eight weights across two separate requests, most of which are never used. The new site uses a three-part system: a technical display face, a screen-optimised body face, and a monospaced face for data and drawing-style annotations, all self-hosted so text appears instantly and never shifts as the page loads. Research on typography and credibility is unambiguous: the same words are judged more trustworthy in one typeface than another, and harder-to-read type makes a task feel harder before anyone has begun it.9,10 A procurement director will not notice the letterforms; they will notice that one site felt effortless and precise.

Three points to settle

These are factual questions we cannot answer for you, and each would be noticed by a client who checks.

1. Were you founded in 2003 or 2008?

Your form gives the founding year as 2008, and the website now shows that. But the company story you wrote says “Since 2003…” and your approved Terms of Use says “established in early 2003”. The site therefore says 2008 in one place and 2003 in two others.

Suggested wording: “Operating since 2003, incorporated in 2008” — which is accurate on both counts. Please confirm.

2. Which CIN is correct?

Your two forms give different numbers: U24319HR2008PTC037858 and U74140HR2008PTC037858. The company number, state and year match; only the activity code differs. We have published the second, because 74140 denotes consultancy whereas 24319 denotes chemical manufacturing — but one of the two is a typing error, and a wrong CIN in the footer is a compliance matter.

3. Your Privacy Policy is missing section 12

The approved text runs from section 11 straight to section 13. We have published it exactly as supplied and have not renumbered anything. Please check whether a clause was lost.
