The one-sentence case

Research says visitors judge a firm's credibility in 50 milliseconds,1 that 75% of users judge company credibility from website design,2 and that B2B buyers now complete roughly two-thirds of their evaluation digitally before ever talking to a supplier.12 For most EPC prospects, strucon.net is the first impression and the shortlist decision — and today it presents 20+ years of engineering excellence through a 2018-era template with broken stat counters.

02 · Methodology & honesty notes

How this report was produced

  • Current site: live audit of strucon.net — HTTP headers, full homepage HTML, font/script/stylesheet inventory, asset weights, robots/sitemap probes, and inner-page checks.
  • New site: measured from the production build (44 routes, static/SSG output) and its verified behaviors — admin save-to-live cycle, form validation, schema output.
  • Research figures are from published industry studies (cited inline, listed in Sources). They quantify directional effects observed across many sites — strong evidence, not guarantees for any single site.
  • Performance: the new site is engineered for 95+ Lighthouse (static HTML, self-hosted fonts, AVIF/WebP, minimal JS). Final scores should be validated on production hosting — this report does not claim a measured score before that.

03 · First impressions & trust

Design is not decoration — it is the credibility audit every visitor runs

Stanford's Web Credibility Project — still the largest study of its kind (2,684 participants) — found that when people assess whether a company can be trusted, the look and design of the site is the single most-cited factor, ahead of the actual information on it.2 A companion study on advice sites found 94% of user comments explaining mistrust were about design — layout, typography, clutter — not content.3

What the 50-millisecond judgment sees today

✕ Current strucon.net

  • Generic construction template (buildpro theme, WPBakery page builder) — indistinguishable from thousands of small-contractor sites.
  • Animated stat counters that render as "0" Hours of Work, "0" Clients, "0" Staff — the homepage literally displays zeros next to trust claims.
  • ISO 9001:2008 on the masthead — a standard withdrawn in September 2018. To a procurement auditor this reads as a lapsed certification.
  • Footer: "Copyright 2023" — signals an unmaintained property.
  • Stock slider carousel with 2019-era imagery.

✓ New platform

  • A proprietary "engineering drawing" identity — blueprint grids, discipline codes (STR/DTL/BIM), title-block data strips. It could belong to no one but a structural engineering firm.
  • Working stats bar, hero title-block with founding year, tonnage, and certification.
  • Case studies in the format EPC buyers actually evaluate: Challenge → Solution → Results with tonnage, schedule, and code metrics.
  • Every trust claim placeholder is flagged for verification before go-live — nothing false ships.
  • Consistent CTAs on all 25+ pages, so credibility converts instead of evaporating.

Why this matters for EPC specifically: Gartner finds B2B buying groups spend only 17% of their journey meeting potential suppliers — the rest is independent research, mostly online.12 McKinsey reports ~70–80% of B2B decision-makers now prefer remote or digital self-serve interactions.13 When a Middle-East EPC engineer shortlists steel detailers at 11pm, the website is the meeting. The current site loses that meeting in 50 milliseconds.

04 · Typography — the deep dive

Font-by-font: what each site says before you read it

Typography research is unambiguous: typefaces measurably change how credible, effortless, and professional content feels. In the New York Times "Baskerville experiment" (~45,000 participants), the same statement earned measurably higher agreement when set in one typeface versus another — readers found identical words more trustworthy purely from the letterforms.5 Song & Schwarz showed that hard-to-process type makes described tasks feel harder and people less willing to act6 — directly relevant when the "task" is engaging an engineering vendor. And an MIT AgeLab / Monotype study found humanist letterforms were processed faster than square-grotesque faces, especially at a glance.7

07 · Lead generation

From brochure to acquisition machine

✕ Current: one exit door

  • A single "Contact Us" page; no CTAs woven through content.
  • Hero is a rotating carousel — Notre Dame's benchmark study found only ~1% of visitors ever click a carousel, and 84% of those clicks are on slide one.16
  • No way to submit a tender, book a review, or request a scoped proposal.
  • No case-study format a buyer can forward to a colleague.

✓ New: five intent-matched paths

  • Request Consultation · Request Proposal · Upload Tender Documents (file upload) · Book Engineering Review · Download Company Profile — each with its own validated form, fields matched to intent.
  • Every one of the 25+ pages ends in a relevant CTA; the navbar carries a persistent consultation button.
  • Case studies structured as Challenge → Solution → Results with metrics — the internal-forwarding format.
  • Client-side + server-side validation (React Hook Form + Zod), graceful success states, and a lead API ready to wire to email/CRM.
$12M

Illustrative funnel math — not a forecast

Suppose search + referrals bring 1,000 relevant visitors/month. Industry-typical B2B service conversion runs ~1–2% on a well-built site and materially lower on a slow, CTA-less one. If the rebuild moves conversion from 0.5% to 1.5% — well within the range the cited speed, trust, and form research supports — that is 5 → 15 qualified enquiries/month. If even one incremental enquiry per quarter becomes a detailing contract, the rebuild pays for itself many times over. The multiplier compounds again as the SEO architecture grows traffic itself.

09 · Accessibility & reach

Small details, larger market

  • WCAG-conscious build: semantic landmarks, skip-to-content link, visible keyboard focus, form labels and error messaging, contrast-checked palette, prefers-reduced-motion respected. The current site's page-builder markup offers none of this systematically.
  • Why it's business, not charity: the WHO estimates ~16% of the world lives with some disability; accessibility overlaps heavily with usability for everyone — including engineers on gloves-and-glare site tablets. Accessible structure is also what search crawlers parse best, so the SEO and accessibility work compound.
  • Global audience: fluid type and mobile-first layout serve the low-bandwidth, small-screen contexts common across EPC site offices — precisely where the 1.33 MB slider payload fails today.

10 · Recommendation

Replace, redirect, verify — in that order

Recommendation: replace strucon.net with the new platform. Every measured dimension — trust signals, typography, payload, search architecture, lead capture, security — favors the rebuild, and the research above prices each gap in lost enquiries.

Go-live conditions (the honest checklist)

  • 301 redirect map from every old URL (/index.php/about/ etc.) to its new equivalent — preserves the domain's existing link equity.
  • Verify before publishing: the stats bar (26+ years / 500+ projects / 100+ engineers / 20 countries), current ISO certification version, real client names/logos with display rights, real leadership bios, and NDA-cleared case-study details. Placeholders are flagged in the build and must not ship as fact.
  • Wire the lead API to email/CRM and file storage; set a strong ADMIN_PASSWORD.
  • Validate Lighthouse/CWV on production hosting and submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console.
  • Keep the hero video ≤ ~3 MB (compressed, muted, with poster) so it never undoes the performance case.

Scope caveat

Research statistics cited here describe effects measured across large samples of other sites; they establish direction and order of magnitude, not a guaranteed outcome for strucon.net. The measured audit facts (versions, payloads, missing tags) are directly verifiable today and are sufficient grounds for the recommendation on their own.
