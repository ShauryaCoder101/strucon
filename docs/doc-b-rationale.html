01 · The thesis

What this website is for

This is not a brochure. It is a lead-generation platform, and every decision in it was made against a single test: does this help an EPC engineer decide to contact STRUCON?

A prospective client arriving on the site is silently asking three questions. The structure answers them in order:

The visitor asksThe site answers with
Can they solve my engineering problem?Eight service pages and ten industry pages, each naming the actual structures involved — pipe racks, preheater towers, conveyor galleries, bunker bays.
Why trust them over another consultant?Ten real case studies with named clients and verified tonnages, ISO 9001:2015, four named directors, and the standards worked to.
How do I start?Five distinct enquiry routes, a call-to-action on every page, and a tender-document upload.

The reason this matters: Gartner finds B2B buying groups spend only 17% of their evaluation time meeting suppliers.1 The rest is independent research, mostly online. For a Middle-East engineer shortlisting steel detailers at 11pm, the website is the first meeting.

02 · Identity

Why the site looks like an engineering drawing

The old site used a purchased construction template. It was indistinguishable from thousands of small-contractor websites, which is the opposite of what a firm selling precision should signal.

Rather than invent a look, the new identity is drawn from STRUCON's own vernacular — the structural steel drawing. The visual devices are not decoration; each one is borrowed from the documents your engineers produce every day:

  • The blueprint grid — a faint coordinate grid behind dark sections, as on a drawing sheet.
  • Title blocks — the strip beneath the homepage headline reading ESTABLISHED / STEEL DETAILED / QUALITY is laid out exactly like the title block in the corner of a fabrication drawing.
  • Discipline codes — each service card carries a three-letter code (STR, DTL, BIM, CIV) in the way drawing sets are indexed.
  • Corner ticks and hairlines — the registration marks of a drawing frame.

Why this is a commercial decision, not an aesthetic one

An engineer recognises this language instantly, and recognition reads as competence. A template cannot do that, because it was designed to fit every industry and therefore belongs to none. The identity is ownable — it could not be lifted onto a competitor's site without looking borrowed.

03 · Typography

Why these three typefaces

Typography is the single largest surface on any website — more of the page is type than anything else — and research shows it measurably changes how credible content feels.

What the old site used, and why it worked against you

Montserrat & Open Sans — current strucon.net
The default pairing shipped with the purchased “buildpro” theme.
Engineering Tomorrow
Open Sans at theme defaults. Among the most-used font combinations on the web, which is precisely the problem: it reads as a bought template even to people who could not name why.

Beyond the familiarity problem, it was loaded badly: eight weights across two separate requests to Google's servers, most of them never used on the page. Every one of those is a round trip the visitor waits through before text settles, and because no metric-matched fallback was defined, the text visibly jumps as the fonts arrive — a scored failure in Google's Core Web Vitals.

What the new site uses

Space Grotesk — display
Headlines and section titles. Used with restraint.
Engineering Excellence Without Boundaries.
A technical grotesque with slightly mechanical, drawn-not-written letterforms. It carries engineering character without tipping into novelty, and it is distinct enough that the site cannot be mistaken for a template.
Inter — body
All running text, descriptions and paragraphs.
Designed specifically for screens: a tall x-height and open apertures keep it legible at small sizes and on the low-brightness screens common in site offices. It is the body face used by GitHub and Figma for exactly this reason.
IBM Plex Mono — data & annotation
Discipline codes, tonnages, labels, title blocks, metrics.
STEEL DETAILED  ·  19,000 MT  ·  AISC  ·  STR / DTL / BIM
The layer that makes the identity work. A monospaced face with engineering heritage, used only for numbers and labels — mirroring the annotations on a fabrication drawing. Its figures are tabular, so tonnages line up in columns.

The three roles, and why a third face exists at all

Most websites use two typefaces. A third is justified here because the content has a third kind of information: not prose and not headline, but data — tonnages, codes, standards, capacities. Giving data its own face does two things. It makes the numbers scannable, and it signals domain fluency, because an engineering audience associates monospaced annotation with drawings and schedules.

DecisionCurrent strucon.netNew website
FacesMontserrat + Open Sans (template default)Space Grotesk + Inter + IBM Plex Mono
Weights loaded8, most unusedOnly those actually used, subset to Latin
Delivery2 external requests that block text renderingSelf-hosted, 0 external requests
Layout shift as fonts loadYes — text visibly jumpsNone — metric-matched fallbacks generated at build
Line lengthUncontrolled by the page builderCapped near 68 characters, the readability optimum
ScaleFixed sizesFluid — type scales smoothly from phone to 4K via clamp()

The evidence that type affects trust

In a New York Times experiment with roughly 45,000 participants, the same statement earned measurably higher agreement purely from the typeface it was set in.2 Song and Schwarz showed that instructions in a harder-to-read font made the described task feel about twice as long, and made people less willing to begin it.3 An MIT AgeLab study measured humanist letterforms being processed faster at a glance than square grotesques.4

A procurement director will never comment on x-heights. They will come away feeling that one site was effortless and precise and the others were noisy — and processing-fluency research shows that feeling transfers onto judgements about the company itself.

The palette comes from the materials: steel is blue-grey, and hot-rolled steel is orange. The navy carries authority and lets photography sit against it without competing; the orange exists for one purpose only.

Discipline: the accent appears almost nowhere

Orange is reserved almost exclusively for calls to action — the buttons that generate enquiries. It is not used for decoration, headings or borders. The result is that on any page, the single most visually arresting element is the thing you want clicked. A palette that uses its brightest colour everywhere teaches the visitor to ignore it.

The neutrals are deliberately biased blue rather than pure grey. A pure mid-grey reads as unconsidered; a grey that leans toward the brand's own hue reads as chosen.

05 · Structure

Why each section exists

No section was added because websites usually have one. Each earns its place by answering a question or removing a doubt.

The homepage, in order

1 · Hero

Answers: who are you and what do you do?

A full-width industrial image, one headline, one supporting line, two buttons. Critically, the headline is live text, not an image — so it appears the instant the page loads rather than waiting for a photograph to download. Research puts first impressions at 50 milliseconds;5 if the headline is inside the image, you have spent that budget before saying anything.

2 · Trusted by

Builds: borrowed credibility, immediately

Larsen & Toubro, Reliance Energy, Jaypee, Punj Lloyd, Paul Wurth, JSW, Enexco. Placed directly beneath the hero because a visitor who recognises one name reads everything afterwards more generously. These are only clients your own case studies name — nothing unconfirmed.

3 · Stats bar

Answers: are you big enough for my project?

Four figures on a dark band — experience, projects, engineers, countries. Set in the display and mono faces so they read like a drawing schedule. Scale is the first disqualifier in EPC procurement, so it appears before anything else can be read.

4 · Services

Answers: can you do the specific thing I need?

Eight cards, each with its discipline code and one plain sentence. The grid is the fastest possible way for a visitor to locate themselves, and each card is a link into a full page — so this section also distributes search authority across the eight service pages.

5 · Industries

Answers: have you worked in my sector?

Ten sector tiles. Sector experience is often a hard requirement in tender prequalification — a cement client wants a cement plant, not general steelwork. Each tile names the real structures: preheater towers, bunker bays, transfer towers.

6 · Featured projects

Proves: everything claimed above

Claims mean little; a 19,000 MT boiler structure for a named client means a great deal. These cards carry client, location, tonnage and standard, because those are the four facts an engineer scans for.

7 · Why STRUCON

Answers: why you and not the other three on my list?

Six differentiators stated plainly. Deliberately unnumbered — the order carries no meaning, and numbering things that are not a sequence is decoration pretending to be structure.

8 · Global presence

Answers: can you serve a project in my country?

Built as an engineering coordinate grid rather than a conventional map — on-brand, and far lighter than a mapping library, which would have cost seconds of load time for a decorative element.

9 · Closing call to action

Converts: the visitor who has read to the end

Someone who reaches the bottom of a homepage is the most qualified visitor you will get that day. The old site let them arrive there and stop.

Why the service pages follow a fixed eight-part template

Every service page runs: Hero → Overview → Benefits → Process → Software → Deliverables → FAQs → Call to action. The order mirrors how a buyer actually evaluates a supplier — what it is, why it matters, how you work, what you use, what I receive, my remaining doubts, how to start.

  • Process is numbered, because it genuinely is a sequence — this is the one place numbering carries information.
  • Software is listed because in detailing, tooling is a qualification: “Tekla” answers a question a procurement form will ask.
  • Deliverables exists because the commonest cause of disputes is a mismatch about what gets handed over. Stating it publicly signals a firm that has had that conversation many times.
  • FAQs remove the last objections before the enquiry form — and they also qualify for enhanced Google listings (see section 06).

Why case studies use Challenge → Solution → Results

This is the format an engineer can forward to a colleague with one line of explanation. It also forces the useful content out: not “we detailed a power plant” but compressed schedule with continuous design revisions → Tekla-led detailing with daily coordination → drawings ahead of erection with zero major RFIs. Metrics appear as a strip — tonnage, schedule, standard — because those are the comparable facts.

Why careers, blog and leadership pages exist at all

  • Leadership — named people with titles convert an anonymous vendor into a firm with accountable individuals. The single largest trust gain available on any B2B site.
  • Careers — doubles as a growth signal. A client checking whether you can staff their project reads an active careers page as evidence that you can.
  • Blog — captures engineers researching a problem rather than a supplier, and demonstrates expertise in your own words. It is also the only part of the site that can grow indefinitely without redesign.

06 · Search

How the site is engineered to be found

The current site is close to invisible to Google — not through bad luck, but because the signals search engines read are absent from it.

SignalCurrent strucon.netNew website
Page titlesOne generic titleUnique per page, written around a commercial search phrase
Meta descriptionsNone on any pageEvery page
SitemapDoes not exist43 URLs, generated automatically
Structured dataNone6 types
Social previewNone — a bare link on LinkedInBranded preview card
Indexable pages~15 thin pages54, each targeting a distinct query

Targeting intent, not volume

The site does not chase the word “engineering”. It targets the phrases typed by someone who already has a tender in hand, and each is given its own page so it can rank:

PagePrimary search phrase
/services/steel-detailingsteel detailing company · Tekla modeling services
/services/bimstructural BIM services · clash detection
/industries/power-energypower plant structural design · boiler structure detailing
/industries/oil-gas-petrochemicalspipe rack design
/industries/material-handling-bulk-solidsconveyor gallery design · transfer tower design

This is why the page count matters. Fifty-four pages is not padding — it is fifty-four separate chances to be the result someone clicks. One page cannot rank for twenty different phrases.

The six structured-data types, and what each buys

Structured data is machine-readable information describing the page to Google. The current site has none, which means it is ineligible for every enhanced result format.

  • Organization and ProfessionalService — establish the company, address and service area; the foundation for appearing as a business entity rather than a loose page.
  • Service — tells Google what each service page actually offers.
  • FAQPage — makes the service-page FAQs eligible for expandable answers directly in search results, which occupy more of the screen than a plain listing.
  • Article — gives blog posts a date and author, enabling news and article treatments.
  • BreadcrumbList — replaces the raw URL in search results with a readable path.

Speed, because Google ranks on it

Core Web Vitals have been part of Google's ranking systems since 2021.6 The new site is built so that the pages are assembled once at build time and served as finished HTML, rather than constructed from a database on every visit as the current WordPress site does. Fonts are self-hosted, images are served in modern compressed formats and only loaded when scrolled to, and the homepage headline is text.

The commercial stake: 53% of mobile visits are abandoned beyond three seconds,7 and Deloitte measured a 0.1-second improvement lifting retail conversion by 8.4%.8 The current homepage asks a first-time visitor to download 39 scripts, 21 stylesheets and 1.33 MB of slider photographs before the page settles.

One deliberate protection

The review site at strucon.vercel.app is set to noindex. Without that, Google could index the preview as a duplicate of your own website and the two would compete. It is removed the moment the site moves to strucon.net.

07 · Trust

How the details compound into credibility

Stanford's Web Credibility Project — 2,684 participants, still the largest study of its kind — found that when people judge whether a company can be trusted, the look and design of the site is the most-cited factor, ahead of the information on it.9 A companion study found 94% of comments explaining mistrust were about design rather than content.10

Trust here is not produced by a single feature. It is the accumulation of small decisions that each say the same thing: this firm is careful.

DecisionWhat a visitor subconsciously concludes
Real client names on real projectsOthers have taken this risk already.
Tonnages, standards and locations statedThey expect to be checked.
Named directors with titlesThere are people here who are accountable.
Deliverables listed publiclyThey have had the scope conversation before.
A stated processDelivery is a system, not improvisation.
Text that never shifts as the page loadsNothing here is broken.
Consistent type, spacing and colour throughoutSomeone was paying attention.

What was deliberately left out — and why that also builds trust

  • No rotating carousel. A University of Notre Dame study found roughly 1% of visitors ever click one, and 84% of those clicks land on the first slide.11 The current site spends its most valuable screen space on a pattern users ignore.
  • No generic stock office photography. Smiling strangers at laptops say nothing about structural engineering and are instantly recognisable as filler.
  • No invented testimonials. None were supplied, so that section says so rather than carrying a plausible fiction.
  • No certification claim until it was verified. The old site displayed ISO 9001:2008 — a standard withdrawn in September 2018. To a procurement auditor that reads as a lapsed certificate. Nothing was published until ISO 9001:2015 was confirmed.
  • No unconfirmed client names. Eight organisations described as “potential” were left off entirely.

Every placeholder still on the site is labelled TBD rather than filled with convincing-sounding filler. Nothing invented will ever be published.

Built to be usable, which overlaps almost entirely with being professional

  • Keyboard focus is visible, a skip-to-content link is present, and every form field has a real label.
  • Colour contrast is checked, so text remains readable on a tablet in daylight on site.
  • Animation is suppressed for visitors who have asked their device to reduce motion.
  • The layout is mobile-first — relevant because site-office browsing is phone-heavy and bandwidth-poor, exactly where the current 1.33 MB homepage fails hardest.

Accessible structure is also the structure search engines parse most reliably, so this work and section 06 reinforce each other.
